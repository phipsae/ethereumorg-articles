# "Decentralized AI on Ethereum" Reference Doc (May 2026)

Compiled 2026-05-26. All numbers carry their source URL and observation date. Where data is contested, sparse, or unverifiable, this is flagged explicitly. Article scope: the DeAI **infrastructure stack** (compute, data, training, inference, verification) and Ethereum's specific role. The agent layer (ERC-8004, x402, ENS for agents) is treated only briefly here because it belongs to the planned sibling article on AI Agents and Ethereum.

## Framing and definitions

**1. What "decentralized AI" means in 2026.** Five overlapping categories, each with different maturity and different Ethereum exposure:
- Decentralized **compute** marketplaces (GPU rental, Kubernetes-style orchestration, settlement via blockchain).
- Decentralized **data** markets and provenance (data DAOs, tokenized datasets, onchain IP licensing).
- Decentralized **training** networks (multi-party model training over the open internet).
- Decentralized **inference** (model serving across permissionless GPUs).
- **Verifiable AI** (cryptographic or economic guarantees that an inference or training step actually happened as claimed).
The article should distinguish these clearly. Most public discussion conflates them, treating "Bittensor up X percent" as a signal about all five, which is misleading.

The investor frame versus the builder frame.** Jake Brukhman of CoinFund argued in CoinDesk on January 31, 2026 that decentralized training creates a new asset class for "tokenized intelligence" where contributors earn ownership in models. Source: https://www.coindesk.com/opinion/2026/01/31/how-decentralized-ai-training-will-create-a-new-asset-class-for-digital-intelligence . Qualifier: this is an investor pitch with undisclosed conflict, CoinFund co-led Pluralis's $7.6M seed in March 2025 per https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/03/19/3045635/0/en/Pluralis-Research-Pioneers-Protocol-Learning-to-Scale-Decentralized-AI-Announces-7-6M-Seed-Round-Led-by-USV-and-CoinFund.html . ## Inference and verifiable AI layer

**28. zkML versus opML versus TEE, the three approaches.**
- **zkML** uses zero-knowledge proofs to cryptographically attest that a model executed correctly on specific inputs. Strong guarantees, slow proof generation, limited model size at the frontier.
- **opML** uses optimistic-rollup-style fraud proofs: claims are accepted unless challenged, with bisection-based dispute resolution. Cheap, supports much larger models, weaker than zkML but stronger than pure trust.
- **TEE-based** approaches use hardware attestations (Intel SGX, AMD SEV, NVIDIA Confidential Computing) to bind a result to an enclave. Fastest, but the trust root is the silicon vendor.
The article should present this as a real engineering tradeoff, not a horse race. Source: https://hackernoon.com/whats-the-difference-between-zkml-and-opml , https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17555 (opML paper) , https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18535 (zkML survey).

Cite the project and approach, not specific throughput numbers, without primary-verifying each.

**38. Honest critique of verifiable AI (2026).**
- zkML proves attractive on paper but cannot yet generate proofs for frontier-scale models in reasonable time. Practical usage is at the scale of small classifiers and quantized 7B-or-smaller LLMs.
- opML scales to larger models but only provides probabilistic security, depends on at least one honest watcher and on the dispute window being long enough to catch fraud.
- TEEs depend on the silicon vendor's threat model. Confidential computing on NVIDIA Hopper or Blackwell shifts the trust root from "this AI lab" to "NVIDIA + the cloud operator hosting the GPU," which is a weaker form of decentralization.
Frame these as engineering choices, not as one being "the answer."

## Ethereum-native primitives that AI applications rely on

**39. EIP-7702 active since Pectra (May 2025).** Set Code for EOAs; lets an externally owned account temporarily delegate to smart-contract code for the duration of a transaction. Authors: Vitalik Buterin, Sam Wilson, Ansgar Dietrichs, lightclient. Source: https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7702 . Pectra activated on Ethereum mainnet 2025-05-07. This is the primitive that makes agent-style flows (a one-shot call into smart-account behavior from a plain EOA) practical.

**40. ERC-8004 is officially "Draft."** The EIP repository labels ERC-8004 ("Trustless Agents") as "⚠️ Draft" with a warning that it is not yet recommended for general use or implementation. Authored August 2025 by Marco De Rossi (MetaMask), Davide Crapis (EF), Jordan Ellis (Google), Erik Reppel (Coinbase). Source: https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8004 . Qualifier: There is a parallel set of secondary reports claiming contracts implementing the ERC have been deployed on mainnet (Jan 29, 2026, ~45k agents across EVM networks per 8004 Scan), and the existing internal research dossier from April 25 confirms those secondary reports. The cleanest framing is: "the standard is still officially Draft; reference implementations have shipped to mainnet and at least one explorer (8004 Scan) reports tens of thousands of agent registrations." Do not say "ERC-8004 is finalized."
